## 1. Course Overview & Philosophy

Welcome to **42620 Science, Technology and Society**. This course equips you with tools and perspectives to understand and critically analyze technology’s impact on society and the environment.

### "Technology for People"
Guided by DTU's strategy of "Technology for people," the course examines the history of science's role in society:
* **Francis Bacon (17th Century):** Science exists "for the relief of man's estate" (to improve human life).
* **J.D. Bernal (1930s):** Research has a social function and should serve society.
* **Vannevar Bush (1940s):** Science is an "endless frontier"; society should invest in basic research to reap unexpected benefits.
* **Mariana Mazzucato (2020s):** Science should be mission-oriented, focusing on 'moonshot' projects to solve grand societal challenges.

### Wicked Problems
The course addresses the challenge that there is no indisputable public good in a pluralistic society. We deal with **Wicked Problems** (Rittel & Webber, 1973) characterized by:
* Unintended and unforeseen societal consequences (positive and negative).
* Disagreement on problem definitions and contested expertise.
* The reality that attempting to delimit a problem effectively involves taking sides.

## 3. Course Structure & Case Study

The course is organized into two consecutive parts. First, you learn to adopt the role of a sustainability expert; then, you deal with the societal reality that other actors—often supported by other experts—may disagree with your assessments.

### Part 1: Sustainability Assessment
* **Focus:** "What is objectively the most sustainable way to build the energy islands?".
* **Method:** Expert-driven approach using life cycle assessment and SDG methodology.
* **Output:** Suggestions for how to improve the sustainability of the energy islands.

### Part 2: Responsible Innovation
* **Focus:** "What will real-world stakeholders... think about our proposals?".
* **Method:** Bottom-up, empirical approach mapping societal debates and analyzing arguments.
* **Output:** A policy brief on how to responsibly implement proposals considering actor disagreements.

### The Case: Danish Energy Islands
We will work with the case of the Danish energy islands. The case material is compiled in **"The Issue Atlas,"** which includes:
* A report on the political, social, and technological context.
* Key documents and a dataset of statements by actors in the public controversy.
* Python scripts for exploring the dataset (available in Part 2).

## 4. Learning Objectives

A student who has met the objectives of the course will be able to:
1.  Establish the significance of SDGs in relation to engineering projects and discover how they can be used in practice.
2.  Know and understand prevailing definitions and the history of the concept of sustainability.
3.  Understand the importance of adopting a life cycle/systems perspective and considering impacts within environment, society, and economy.
4.  Identify how social values are embedded in engineering projects and how technology becomes a matter of public concern.
5.  Describe the role of ethics in engineering work within one’s own field.
6.  Describe and evaluate the primary social processes of designing, implementing, adopting, and contesting innovation.
7.  Discuss key dimensions of responsible research and innovation (anticipation, reflexivity, inclusion, responsiveness).
8.  Recommend responsible strategies to foster absolute social and environmental sustainability.

## 7. Final Exam

The final exam consists of two components. Both are pass/fail and assessed individually.

### 1. Multiple Choice Test (MCT)
* **Format:** Individual, on-site exam.
* **Time:** Between 9:00 and 11:00 on the last day of the course (23/1).
* **Content:** Concepts learned in the lectures of the 2nd week.
* **Aids:** **No aids** are allowed.

### 2. Policy Brief
* **Format:** Written group assignment, but individual responsibility for specific parts must be indicated.
* **Deadline:** Handed in on Learn **before 17:00** on the last day of the course (23/1).
* **Task:** Suggest sustainability improvements, map the controversy, and recommend a strategy for responsible implementation.
* **Supervision:** The last four days (20/1 - 23/1) are dedicated to writing. You will be assigned a supervisor for two collective sessions.
