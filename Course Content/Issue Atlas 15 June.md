# ENERGY ISLANDS: The Issue Atlas
**Case material for sociotechnical controversies**
*June 2025*

## 1. Introduction to the Case: The Danish Energy Islands

In the Danish Government's Climate Agreement for Energy and Industry of 20th June 2020, a broad political majority decided that two so-called energy islands should be constructed: one artificial island located in the North Sea and the other on the natural island of Bornholm in the Baltic Sea.

### Project Scope
* **North Sea Energy Island:** Initially expected to deliver 3 GW offshore wind power, with a long-term goal of 10 GW.
* **Bornholm Energy Island:** Projected to produce 2 GW, and later to be upscaled to 3 GW.
* **Power-to-X (PtX):** The hubs are envisioned to be coupled to PtX facilities to produce hydrogen and green fuels to decarbonize hard-to-abate sectors.

### The Concept
At its core, an Energy Island is a hub designed to connect adjacent offshore wind farms and distribute electricity to surrounding national grids via High Voltage Direct Current (HVDC) transmission lines. Unlike traditional point-to-point cables, these islands are envisioned to connect to multiple countries and other islands in a "hub-and-spoke" concept. 

### Current Status (as of June 2025)
Several uncertainties currently jeopardize the construction of the Energy Islands.
* **Financing Issues:** The original plan relied on income from tendering offshore wind farms, but the first tendering round in December 2024 received zero bids.
* **Project Delays:** The North Sea Energy Island was officially put on hold in June 2023, and the Bornholm Energy Island in January 2025.
* **European Support:** Despite delays, the EU Commission announced a €645 million grant for the Bornholm Energy Island in January 2025, viewing it as a cornerstone of EU net-zero targets.

## 2. Timeline

**Pre-controversy History**
* **1996:** "Energy 21" strategy plan suggests the "Energy Island" concept.
* **1997:** Samsø wins the title of renewable energy island.
* **2016:** TenneT presents the hub-and-spoke concept.
* **2017:** Consortium (Energinet, TenneT, 50Hertz) established for North Sea Energy Hub.
* **2019:** Ørsted suggests Bornholm as a test bed island.

**Active Controversy**
* **June 2020:** Climate Agreement makes Energy Islands official Danish energy policy.
* **February 2021:** Political majority votes for North Sea Island plan (est. cost 210 billion DKK).
* **May 2022:** Esbjerg Declaration signed at international climate meeting.
* **June 2023:** North Sea Energy Island project is put on hold.
* **December 2024:** Historically biggest bidding round results in zero bids.
* **January 2025:** Bornholm Energy Island put on hold awaiting German election results.

## 4. The Technology of Energy Hubs

The massive upscaling of renewable energy requires transporting electrical energy from production sites (far offshore) to consumption sites.
* **HVDC (High Voltage Direct Current):** Essential for long distances as AC causes large energy losses. HVDC reduces losses and eliminates reactive power challenges.
* **The Hub Concept:** Simulations show that combining country interconnections with offshore hubs increases the utility and efficiency of interconnector cables.

**Technical Challenges:**
1.  **Lack of Inertia:** DC systems lack the rotating mass (inertia) of traditional AC thermal power stations, making control and communication critical for balancing generation and consumption.
2.  **Fault Sensitivity:** DC converters are sensitive to current spikes and fault handling is difficult compared to robust AC generators.
3.  **Circuit Breakers:** In AC systems, the voltage naturally passes through zero (zero-crossing), making it safer to break the current. DC systems have no zero point, requiring breakers to dissipate vast energy instantly—a technology still in its infancy.

## 5. Dataset & Methodology

The atlas is based on a dataset of **6,061 actor statements** collected between January and May 2025.
* **Collection:** Systematic web search of media, parliamentary debates, social media, and reports from 1998 to 2025.
* **Processing:** Statements were paraphrased into first-person utterances, translated to English, and anonymized if from private citizens.
* **Classification:** Issues were identified through expert-driven classification (16 defined issues) and bottom-up semantic analysis (clustering).
