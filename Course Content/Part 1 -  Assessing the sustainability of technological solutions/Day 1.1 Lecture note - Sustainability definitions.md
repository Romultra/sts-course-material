# Day 1.1: Sustainability Definitions

## Core Definition: The Brundtland Commission (1987)

> **Sustainable Development**: *"...meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs"*

This is the foundational definition you must know. Key aspects:
- **Intergenerational equity**: Present actions must not harm future generations
- **Needs-based framing**: Focus on meeting human needs, not wants
- **Balance**: Requires balancing current development with long-term preservation

---

## The Three Dimensions of Sustainability (Triple Bottom Line)

Three dimensions interact in the creation of sustainable solutions:

| Dimension | Focus |
|-----------|-------|
| **Environment** | Natural resources, ecosystems, climate, biodiversity |
| **Society** | Human wellbeing, equity, health, education, justice |
| **Economy** | Financial viability, growth, employment, trade |

### Two Visual Models

**1. Venn Diagram Model (Traditional)**
- Three overlapping circles (Environment, Economy, Society)
- Sustainability exists at the intersection of all three
- Implies equal weight and trade-offs between dimensions

**2. Nested/Concentric Model (Hierarchical)**
- Earth's life support system (outermost)
- Human societies (middle)
- Economies (innermost)
- **Key insight**: Economy is embedded within society, which is embedded within the environment
- Implies environmental limits are absolute constraints

## Historical Evolution: From MDGs to SDGs

### Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) - 2000-2015

**8 goals** to help the poorest achieve better living standards by 2015:

1. Eradicate extreme poverty and hunger
2. Achieve universal primary education
3. Promote gender equality and empower women
4. Reduce child mortality
5. Improve maternal health
6. Combat HIV/AIDS, malaria and other diseases
7. Ensure environmental sustainability
8. Global partnership for development

**Limitation**: Primarily focused on developing countries and poverty reduction

### Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) - 2015-2030

**17 goals** to *"end poverty, protect the planet, and ensure prosperity for all as part of a new sustainable development agenda"*

**Key differences from MDGs**:
- Universal (applies to ALL countries, not just developing)
- Holistic perspective (integrates all three sustainability dimensions)
- More comprehensive (17 goals vs 8)

## The 17 SDGs Categorized

### By Theme (People, Planet, Prosperity)

| Category | SDGs | Focus |
|----------|------|-------|
| **People** | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | Basic human needs and dignity |
| **Planet** | 6, 13, 14, 15 | Environmental protection |
| **Prosperity** | 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 | Economic growth and infrastructure |
| **Peace & Partnership** | 16, 17 | Governance and collaboration |

### Technology-Driven Goals
Several SDGs are explicitly technology-driven:
- SDG 7: Affordable and Clean Energy
- SDG 9: Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure
- SDG 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities
- SDG 12: Responsible Consumption and Production

## SDG Structure: Goals, Targets, and Indicators

| Level | Count | Purpose |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **Goals** | 17 | Broad aspirational objectives |
| **Targets** | 169 | Specific, measurable outcomes |
| **Indicators** | 232 | Metrics to track progress |

| Target | Description | Indicator |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| 12.1 | Implement 10-Year Framework on Sustainable Consumption and Production | Number of countries with SCP national action plans |
| 12.2 | By 2030, achieve sustainable management and efficient use of natural resources | Material footprint per capita |
| 12.3 | By 2030, halve per capita global food waste at retail and consumer levels | Global food loss index |

## SDG Impacts and Levers Framework

**Source**: Katherine Richardson (2020) - "How do we create sustainable development for all?"

### Two Impact Areas

| Area | SDGs | Description |
|------|------|-------------|
| **Global Environmental Commons** | 13, 14, 15 | Climate, oceans, land ecosystems |
| **Human Wellbeing** | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 | Poverty, hunger, health, education, gender, water |

### Linking Goal
- **SDG 10: Reduced Inequalities** - Connects environmental commons to human wellbeing

### Four Levers for Transformation

| Lever | Associated SDGs | Mechanism |
|-------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Science and Technology** | 7, 9, 11, 12 | Innovation and technical solutions |
| **Economics and Finance** | 8, 17 | Investment and economic incentives |
| **Governance** | 16, 17 | Policy, institutions, partnerships |
| **Individual and Collective Action** | 11, 12, 17 | Behavior change and community action |

## The Role of Engineers in Sustainability

### Key Responsibilities

1. **Integrate sustainability thinking** into all engineering work
2. **Address all relevant goals** - not just one or two
3. **Take a systems perspective** on technology
4. **Identify synergies and antagonisms** between goals

### Important Concepts

- **Synergy**: When progress on one goal supports progress on another
  - Example: Clean energy (SDG 7) supports climate action (SDG 13)

- **Antagonism**: When progress on one goal hinders another
  - Example: Economic growth (SDG 8) may conflict with environmental protection (SDG 13-15)

### The Holistic Perspective
- The topics addressed by SDGs are OLD (poverty, environment, health)
- What is NEW is the **holistic, integrated perspective**
- Engineers must consider interconnections and trade-offs
