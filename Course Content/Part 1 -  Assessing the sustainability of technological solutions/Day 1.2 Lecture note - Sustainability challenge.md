## Core Concept: The Holocene and Sustainability

The sustainability challenge is fundamentally about **keeping the planet in the Holocene** - the stable geological epoch (last ~10,000 years) during which human civilization developed. The Holocene provided stable climate conditions that enabled agriculture and civilization.

---

## The IPAT Equation (Key Formula)

### Definition
**I = P x A x T**

| Variable | Meaning | Measurement |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| **I** | Environmental Impact | Total environmental burden |
| **P** | Population | Number of people |
| **A** | Affluence | Material standard of living (GDP/person) |
| **T** | Technology Factor | Environmental intensity (Impact/GDP) |

### Key Scholars
- **Ehrlich & Holdren (1971)** - "Impact of population growth" - Science
- **Commoner (1972)** - "The environmental cost of economic growth"
- **Graedel & Allenby (1995)** - Industrial Ecology

### The Challenge Quantified (Climate Change 2020-2030)
Using IPAT to calculate required technology improvement:
- **Impact (I):** Must reduce to 0.5 (50% of current emissions by 2030 for 1.5C target)
- **Population (P):** Expected to increase by factor of 1.1
- **Affluence (A):** Expected to increase by factor of 1.3

**T = 0.5 / (1.1 x 1.3) = 0.35**

This means technology must improve by **Factor 3** (reduce impact to ~35% of current levels) by 2030 just for climate change.

## Planetary Boundaries Framework

### Definition
**Planetary Boundaries** define the "safe operating space" for humanity - thresholds beyond which Earth's systems may become destabilized.

### Key Scholars
- **Steffen, Richardson, Rockstrom et al. (2015)** - "Planetary boundaries: Guiding human development on a changing planet" - Science
- **Richardson et al. (2023)** - "Earth beyond six of nine planetary boundaries" - Science Advances

### The Nine Planetary Boundaries
1. Climate change (CO2 concentration, radiative forcing)
2. Novel entities (chemical pollution)
3. Stratospheric ozone depletion
4. Atmospheric aerosol loading
5. Ocean acidification
6. Biogeochemical flows (N and P cycles)
7. Freshwater change (blue and green water)
8. Land-system change
9. Biosphere integrity (genetic and functional diversity)

**Current Status:** As of 2023, six of nine boundaries have been exceeded.

## Eco-Efficiency

### Definition
**Eco-efficiency** = Delivered Service / Environmental Impact = **1/T**

Eco-efficiency is the **reciprocal of the Technology factor** in the IPAT equation.

### Key Principle
"Improved eco-efficiency means **creating more with less**"

### Requirements for Quantifying Eco-efficiency
A **systems perspective** is needed:
1. **Life cycle perspective** - to avoid problem-shifting between life stages
2. **All relevant impact types** - not just carbon
3. **Trade-offs** between impacts and sustainability dimensions

### Stages

5. End-of-life (recycling, disposal, waste treatment)

### Environmental Exchanges
Throughout the life cycle, processes exchange with surroundings:
- **Inputs:** Resources and materials
- **Outputs:** Products, emissions, and waste

### Regional Scale

## Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)

### Definition
**LCA** is the tool to quantify eco-efficiency. It:
- Fixes the delivered service by focusing on a **functional unit**
- Quantifies environmental impacts per delivered service
- Examines the **whole life cycle** and **all relevant environmental impacts**

### The Person Equivalent (PE)
A common metric for comparing environmental impacts:

**How large is the impact from the product compared to the annual environmental impact from an average person?**

| Impact Category | Europe (per person/year) | World (per person/year) |
|-----------------|-------------------------|------------------------|
| Climate change | 9.2 ton CO2-eq | 8.1 ton CO2-eq |
| Ozone depletion | 0.022 kg CFC-11-eq | 0.041 kg CFC-11-eq |
| Freshwater eutrophication | 1.49 kg P eq | 0.62 kg P eq |
| Water depletion | 256 m3 | 395 m3 |

## The Rebound Effect

### Definition
The **rebound effect** occurs when consumption (A) and efficiency (T) are **not independent** - improvements in efficiency can drive increased consumption, meaning **A x T remains constant or grows**, leading to higher total impact.

### Example: Lighting Technology
- Efficiency improved dramatically: Candle (0.01 Lumen/W) to LED (>100 Lumen/W) - **10,000x improvement**
- Yet global energy consumption for lighting remained at **~0.72% of GDP** for 300 years
- As lighting became cheaper, people simply used more light

### Implication
Efficiency improvements alone may not reduce absolute environmental impact due to increased consumption.

## Relative vs. Absolute Sustainability

### Relative Sustainability
- "More sustainable than..." comparisons
- Same or higher functionality with less environmental impact
- **LCA supports relative assessments**

### Absolute Sustainability
- "Sustain-able" - within planetary limits
- Identifies boundaries beyond which activities become unsustainable
- Requires **absolute metrics** to prevent greenwashing

### The Problem with Relative Metrics
A product can be "more sustainable" than alternatives but still be **absolutely unsustainable** (e.g., a biofuel Hummer is "greener" than a gasoline Hummer, but still unsustainable).

## Absolute Sustainability Framework

### Pollution Space Concept
- Respect environmental limits (planetary boundaries)
- Assign "pollution space" to activities
- Total pollution space is finite and must be shared

### Sustainable Per Capita Impacts (Key Data)

| Impact Category | Current Impact | Sustainable Impact |
|-----------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Climate change | 8.1 ton CO2-eq | **0.98 ton CO2-eq** |
| Photochemical ozone | 57 kg NMVOC-eq | 2.5 kg NMVOC-eq |
| Land use (soil quality) | 9 tons eroded soil | 1.2 tons eroded soil |

**Key Insight:** For climate, current per-capita impact is **8x the sustainable level**.

### Three Perspectives on Sustainable Space
1. **Consumer perspective:** Personal impact budget - "How much of my environmental space does this product occupy?"
2. **Company perspective:** Portfolio allocation - "What space can we allow in our product portfolio?"
3. **Societal perspective:** Command and control - "What space can we allocate to this technology?"

## Science Based Targets Initiative (SBTi)

### Definition
Established in 2015, SBTi sets targets "in line with what the latest climate science says is necessary to meet the goals of the Paris Agreement" - limiting warming to **well-below 2C** and pursuing **1.5C**.

### Partners
- CDP (Carbon Disclosure Project)
- UN Global Compact
- WWF
- World Resources Institute

### Growth
- As of January 2026: **12,353 businesses** with targets and commitments
- **9,674** with science-based targets
- **2,325** with net-zero targets

### The Three Scopes of Emissions
| Scope | Type | Examples |
|-------|------|----------|
| **Scope 1** | Direct emissions | Company facilities, company vehicles |
| **Scope 2** | Indirect (energy) | Purchased electricity, steam, heating, cooling |
| **Scope 3** | Indirect (value chain) | Upstream: purchased goods, transport, business travel; Downstream: use of sold products, end-of-life treatment |

### Five Steps for Companies
1. **Commit** - Submit letter of intent
2. **Develop** - Create emissions reduction target
3. **Submit** - Present target for SBTi validation
4. **Communicate** - Announce target to stakeholders
5. **Disclose** - Report emissions and progress annually

## Key Takeaways for Exam

1. **Eco-efficiency is necessary but not sufficient** - must be combined with absolute sustainability framing

2. **Remaining space up to planetary boundaries is a scarce resource** that must be shared

3. **Life cycle perspective is essential** to avoid problem-shifting

4. **Rebound effects can neutralize efficiency gains** - efficiency alone does not guarantee reduced impact

5. **Absolute sustainability:**
   - Guides everyday consumer decisions
   - Offers essential long-term perspective for strategic decisions

6. **Full sustainability requires addressing all three dimensions:** environmental, social, and economic (all relevant SDGs)
