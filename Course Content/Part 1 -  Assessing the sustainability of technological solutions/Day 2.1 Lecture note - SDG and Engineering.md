## 1. Core Definitions

### What is an Engineer?
**Definition (Wikipedia):** Engineers design materials, structures, and systems while considering the limitations imposed by practicality, regulation, safety, and cost.

**Etymology:** The word "engineer" derives from Latin:
- *ingeniator* = to contrive, devise
- *ingenium* = cleverness

### DTU's Mission (HC Orsted, 1829)
> "DTU will develop and create value using the natural sciences and the technical sciences **to benefit society**."

**Key Insight:** The SDGs help qualify what "to benefit society" means in practice.

## 2. The Evolution of Sustainability Thinking

### Three Views of Sustainability

| View | Description | Visualization |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| **Unconnected/Silos** | Economy, Society, Environment treated separately | Three separate circles |
| **Interconnected/Linkages** | Overlapping concerns with shared areas | Venn diagram with overlaps |
| **Interdependent/Nested/Systems** | Economy within Society within Environment | Concentric circles (Environment largest) |

### Brundtland Definition (1987)
> "Humanity has the ability to make development sustainable to ensure that it meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs."

## 5. Engineering's Role in Society

### Public Perception of Engineering (QEPrize 2015 Survey, n=10,341)

**Agreement that engineering innovations have driven progress:**
- Global average: 82% (past) / 85% (future)
- Highest: India (87%/89%)
- Lowest: Japan (75%/78%)

### Shifting Role of Engineering

| Rank | Role TODAY | % | Role in NEXT 20 YEARS | % |
|------|------------|---|----------------------|---|
| 1st | Inspire new innovations | 59% | **Solve world's problems** | 57% |
| 2nd | Improve quality of life | 58% | Inspire new innovations | 55% |
| 3rd | Help economy grow | 57% | Improve quality of life | 55% |
| 7th | Solve world's problems | 42% | Provide job opportunities | 49% |

**Key Shift:** "Solving the world's problems" moves from 7th to 1st place in future expectations.

## 9. Planetary Boundaries Framework

### The Nine Planetary Boundaries (Rockstrom et al., 2009)

| Boundary | Status |
|----------|--------|
| Climate change | Zone of uncertainty (increasing risk) |
| Biosphere integrity (genetic) | Beyond zone (high risk) |
| Biosphere integrity (functional) | Not yet quantified |
| Land-system change | Zone of uncertainty |
| Freshwater use | Below boundary (safe) |
| Biogeochemical flows (Phosphorus) | Beyond zone (high risk) |
| Biogeochemical flows (Nitrogen) | Beyond zone (high risk) |
| Ocean acidification | Below boundary (safe) |
| Stratospheric ozone depletion | Below boundary (safe) |
| Novel entities | Not yet quantified |
| Atmospheric aerosol loading | Not yet quantified |

**Color Coding:**
- **Green:** Below boundary (safe)
- **Yellow/Orange:** In zone of uncertainty (increasing risk)
- **Red:** Beyond zone of uncertainty (high risk)
- **Grey:** Boundary not yet quantified

## 10. A Good Life for All Within Planetary Boundaries

### The Challenge (O'Neill et al., 2018)

**Key Finding:** No country currently achieves a good life for its citizens within planetary boundaries.

**Country Comparison:**
| Country | Social Thresholds Achieved | Biophysical Boundaries Transgressed |
|---------|---------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Vietnam | ~6 | ~1 |
| Sri Lanka | ~3 | ~1 |
| Denmark | ~10-11 | ~6 |
| USA | ~10-11 | ~7 |

**Social Indicators:** Life Satisfaction, Healthy Life Expectancy, Nutrition, Sanitation, Income, Access to Energy, Education, Social Support, Democratic Quality, Equality, Employment

**Dilemma:** Countries with high social achievement tend to transgress more planetary boundaries.

---

## 11. Scenarios for Achieving SDGs Within Planetary Boundaries

### Four Scenarios (Randers et al., 2018)

| Scenario | Description | SDG Progress | Planetary Boundary Impact |
|----------|-------------|--------------|---------------------------|
| **SAME** | Business as Usual | Moderate increase | Continues transgressing |
| **FASTER** | Higher economic growth | Better SDG scores | Worse planetary impact |
| **HARDER** | Trying harder on all fronts | Good SDG progress | Moderate improvement |
| **SMARTER** | Bold, transformational policies | Best SDG achievement | Returns to safe space |

**Key Insight:** Only the SMARTER scenario achieves SDGs within planetary boundaries by 2050.

## 13. The Gap: Importance vs. Ability vs. Action

### Comparative Analysis (Thuesen 2020)

|--------|-----------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| Economy | 70% | 61% | 39% |
| Society | 80% | 57% | 40% |

**Critical Gap:** Environmental SDGs are rated most important and engineering is seen as highly capable, yet only 19% of projects address them.

## 14. Global Support for SDGs

### UN Global Compact
- **Definition:** Voluntary initiative based on CEO commitments to implement universal sustainability principles
- **Participation:** 9,000+ companies in 170 countries
- **Growth:** From 44 business participants (2000) to 8,041 (2015)

**Danish Member Companies:** LEGO, Rockwool, Velux, MT Hojgaard, KMD, HOFOR, Danish Crown, Vestas, Maersk, DONG, Microsoft

### C40 Cities Initiative
- **Scope:** 80+ megacities, 600 million people, 25% of global economy
- **Focus:** Climate change mitigation and adaptation
- **Commitment:** 1.5C Paris Agreement target
- **Actions:** 10,000 climate actions, committed to reducing 3 Gt CO2e by 2030

## 15. Why Engineers Should Care About SDGs

### Four Key Reasons

1. **Obligation:** We have a responsibility to benefit society
2. **Motivation:** Purpose (along with Mastery and Autonomy) drives motivation
3. **Collaboration:** SDGs enable cross-disciplinary and cross-organizational work
4. **Direction:** SDGs help answer:
   - What legacy do I want to leave?
   - What projects should I engage in?

## 16. Key Takeaways for Exam

### Must-Know Concepts

1. **SDG Structure:** 17 Goals, 169 Targets, 304 Indicators (vs. MDGs: 8/21/60)
2. **Three Sustainability Views:** Silos, Linkages, Nested/Systems
3. **Brundtland Definition:** Meeting present needs without compromising future generations
4. **Engineering's Shifting Role:** From "inspiring innovation" to "solving world's problems"
5. **Planetary Boundaries:** 9 boundaries, several already transgressed
6. **SDG-Engineering Gap:** High importance + high capability =/= high action (especially environmental)

### Critical Numbers
- SDGs adopted: 25 September 2015
- UN member states: 193 (unanimous adoption)
- Denmark's SDG ranking: #3 globally
- Engineering can solve clean energy challenges: 91%
- Environmental SDGs addressed by projects: only 19%

### Key Frameworks
- **Stockholm Resilience Centre "Wedding Cake":** Biosphere > Society > Economy
- **Planetary Boundaries:** Safe operating space for humanity
- **O'Neill et al. Model:** Social thresholds vs. biophysical boundaries
