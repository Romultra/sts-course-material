# SDG Methodology: Phase 2 (Scoping) and Phase 3 (Identifying Effects)

## Overview of the SDG Methodology

### Purpose
To assess the contribution from a project or technology shift on the SDGs **semi-quantitatively** and in a **life cycle perspective**.

> "What gets measured gets managed!"

### The Five Phases

| Phase | Name | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| **Phase 1** | Selection of Application | Selecting the span of the case project |
| **Phase 2** | Scoping | Defining system boundaries for baseline and new system |
| **Phase 3** | Identifying and Categorizing Effects | Finding differences between systems |
| **Phase 4** | Assessment | Evaluating effect's impact on SDGs |
| **Phase 5** | Interpretation | Recommendations based on findings |

**Key Feature:** The methodology is **iterative** - you can go back between phases as understanding develops.

### Three Sustainability Dimensions
- **Environment**
- **Society**
- **Economy**

---

## Phase 1 Recap: Selection of Application

### The Technology Shift Framework
Every SDG assessment compares **two systems**:

| BEFORE the change | AFTER the change |
|-------------------|------------------|
| **The BASELINE system** | **The NEW system** |

### The Functional Output (Critical Concept)

**Definition:** The function that both systems must deliver to enable fair comparison.

**Example - Shopping Bags:**
- Wrong comparison: "One plastic bag vs. one cloth bag"
- Correct comparison: "Carriage of 10 litres of products for 100 metres"

**Why it matters:** The functional output must be **equivalent** between systems to make a fair sustainability comparison.

### Three Considerations for Functional Output
1. **Time** - When does the function occur?
2. **Application** - How is the function used?
3. **Geography** - Where does the function take place?

## Phase 2: Scoping the Assessment

### Definition
**Scoping** = Determining how much to include in the study of the case

### Learning Goals for Phase 2
1. Understand and scope the system described in the case
2. Identify and present all relevant processes in the life cycle
3. Seek knowledge and select a starting point for data collection

### The Life Cycle Perspective (Key Framework)

The life cycle provides **structure** for organizing processes into four stages:

| Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Stage 3 | Stage 4 |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Materials and Components** | **Production** | **Use and Maintenance** | **Reuse, Recycling and Discarding** |

### How to Scope: The Iteration Process

**Problem:** Systems can be infinitely complex with interconnected processes.

**Solution:** Scope **by iteration** - start broad, then refine boundaries based on what matters for the comparison.

### Creating Process Diagrams

**Process Diagram Structure:**
```
Materials &        Production       Use &           Recycling &
Components                          Maintenance     Discarding
    |                  |               |                |
[Process 1] -----> [Process 5] ---> [Process 8] ---> [Process 9]
[Process 2] -----> [Process 6]                   ---> [Process 10]
[Process 3] -----> [Process 7]                   ---> [Process 11]
[Process 4]
```

### Process Diagram Examples

**Example 1: Electric Vehicle System**
- Materials: Mining for metals, extraction of fossil resources, mining for minerals
- Production: Production of EV batteries, refining of fossil fuels, construction of roads
- Use: Use of cars, maintenance of roads
- End-of-life: Disassembling of cars, recycling of EV batteries, recycling of car tyres

**Example 2: House Construction**
- Materials: Extraction of sand for glass, mining minerals for concrete, mining calcium for cement, extraction of fossil resources for fuel
- Production: Production of windows, production of concrete, production of cement, production of fuel for heating
- Use: Use of house
- End-of-life: Recycling of glass, recycling of concrete, combustion of wood parts

### Comparing Baseline and New System Processes

When creating the NEW system diagram, mark changes compared to BASELINE:

| Change Type | Notation | Color Code |
|-------------|----------|------------|
| **Added process** | New in NEW system | Blue/Purple |
| **Removed process** | Was in BASELINE, not in NEW | Grey/Faded |
| **Increased process** | Same process, larger scale | Green/Orange |
| **Reduced process** | Same process, smaller scale | Green/Orange |
| **No change** | Identical in both systems | Standard |

### Phase 2 Deliverables
1. **Definition and scoping** of both the BASELINE and NEW system
2. **Process diagram** for each system showing:
   - All relevant processes organized by life cycle stage
   - Clear indication of which processes are in which system

## Phase 3: Identifying and Categorizing Effects

### Definition
**Effects** = The differences between the BASELINE system and the NEW system

### Learning Goals for Phase 3
1. Understand the difference between processes and effects
2. Identify physical and non-physical effects
3. Identify direct and indirect effects

### Two Classification Dimensions for Effects

#### Dimension 1: Direct vs. Indirect Effects

| **DIRECT EFFECTS** | **INDIRECT EFFECTS** |
|--------------------|---------------------|
| Directly caused by the change | Often unexpected consequences |
| Reflects the **purpose** of the change | Impact other products, processes, or systems |
| Intended outcomes | Unintended outcomes |

**Example - Replacing Fossil Energy with Renewables:**
- **Direct effects:** Reduced CO2 emissions, reduced fossil fuel extraction
- **Indirect effects:** Increased demand for rare earth minerals, changes in employment patterns in energy sector, need for new grid infrastructure

#### Dimension 2: Physical vs. Non-Physical Effects

| **PHYSICAL EFFECTS** | **NON-PHYSICAL EFFECTS** |
|---------------------|-------------------------|
| Direct changes in physical conditions | Changes in social aspects |
| Can be measured/quantified | Often qualitative |
| Material flows, emissions, energy use | Behavior, working conditions, education, equality |

**Classification Examples:**

| Effect | Type |
|--------|------|
| Lower extraction of minerals for cement production | **Physical** |
| Workers feeling more secure in the work space | **Non-physical** |
| More plastic is being recycled | **Physical** |
| Lower fuel consumption for transportation | **Physical** |
| Increased accessibility for people with disabilities | **Non-physical** |
| Altered education need from new technology | **Non-physical** |

### Process to Effect Conversion

Effects emerge from **changes in processes** between systems:

| Process Change | Effect Description |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Process added in NEW system | Effect: New process added (+) |
| Process removed from BASELINE | Effect: Process removed (-) |
| Process increased in NEW system | Effect: Process increased (arrow up) |
| Process reduced in NEW system | Effect: Process reduced (arrow down) |
| No change | No effect generated |

### Effect Tables Structure

**Physical Effects Table:**

| Materials & Components | Production | Use & Maintenance | Recycling & End-of-Life |
|----------------------|------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| Effect 1: Process 2 increased | Effect 5: Process 6 removed | Effect 8: Process 14 added | Effect 10: Process 9 removed |
| Effect 2: Process 3 removed | Effect 6: Process 7 reduced | Effect 9: Non-physical effect | Process 10: no change |
| Effect 3: Process 4 removed | Effect 7: Process 13 added | Process 8: no change | Effect 11: Process 15 added |
| Effect 4: Process 12 added | Process 5: no change | | Process 11: no change |

**Non-Physical Effects Table:**
- Separate table for effects related to social behavior, working conditions, education, equality, etc.

### Iteration Between Phase 2 and Phase 3

**Important:** Phase 2 and Phase 3 are highly connected:
- While identifying effects (Phase 3), you may discover missing processes
- Return to Phase 2 to add those processes
- Then continue with Phase 3

This iterative loop continues until the assessment is complete.

### Phase 3 Deliverables
1. **Effect tables** showing all physical and non-physical effects
2. **Classification** of each effect:
   - Direct or indirect
   - Physical or non-physical
   - Life cycle stage location
   - Change type (+, -, increased, reduced)

## Practical Example: Beercycling Case

### Case Description
- **Baseline:** Traditional Tuborg beer production with conventional fertilizer
- **New system:** "Pisner" beer - 54,000L urine collected at Roskilde Festival (2015), used as alternative fertilizer for 11 tons of barley (2016)

### What to Identify
- All processes in conventional beer production (BASELINE)
- Additional/changed processes for urine-fertilized beer (NEW)
- Effects: What changes when switching from synthetic to urine-based fertilizer?

## Key Definitions for Exam

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Functional Output** | The equivalent function that both baseline and new systems must deliver |
| **Life Cycle Perspective** | Considering all stages from materials through end-of-life |
| **Scoping** | Determining system boundaries for assessment |
| **Process** | An activity within the system (e.g., mining, production, use) |
| **Effect** | A change/difference between baseline and new system |
| **Direct Effect** | Change that reflects the intended purpose |
| **Indirect Effect** | Unintended consequence affecting other systems |
| **Physical Effect** | Measurable change in physical conditions |
| **Non-Physical Effect** | Change in social/behavioral aspects |
| **Technology Shift** | The change from baseline to new system |

---

## Quick Reference: Phase 2 vs Phase 3

| Aspect | Phase 2: Scoping | Phase 3: Effects |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| **Question** | What processes are in each system? | What changes between systems? |
| **Output** | Process diagrams | Effect tables |
| **Focus** | Mapping the systems | Identifying differences |
| **Tool** | Draw.io/PowerPoint | SDGUI |

## Common Exam Topics

1. **Explain the difference between direct and indirect effects** with examples
2. **Classify effects** as physical or non-physical
3. **Create a process diagram** for a given technology scenario
4. **Identify the functional output** for comparing two systems
5. **Explain why iteration** between phases is necessary
6. **Apply the four life cycle stages** to organize processes
