## Phase 4: Assessment of the Impacts of the Effects on the SDGs

### Overview

Phase 4 has **two main steps**:

| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Step 1** | Link the effects to the SDGs (directly or via consequences) |
| **Step 2** | Semi-quantitative assessment of each consequence |

---

### Step 1: Link Effects to SDGs via Causality Chains

**The Causality Chain Structure:**

```
Effects --> Consequences --> SDG Impacts
```

**Key Concept:** Effects identified in Phase 3 must be linked to the SDGs. This can happen:
- **Directly:** Effect links straight to an SDG
- **Via consequences:** Effect leads to intermediate consequences that then impact SDGs

#### Causality Chain Diagram

```
Effect A --> Consequence 1 ---------> SDG Z
         |                    |
         --> Consequence 2 ---+------> SDG X
                    |
                    --> Consequence N --> SDG Y
```

#### Example: Causality Chain for Metal Mining

**Effect:** Increased mining for a specific metal

**Pathway through small-scale mining:**

| Effect | Intermediate Steps | Consequences | SDG Impacts |
|--------|-------------------|--------------|-------------|
| Increased mining | Small scale mining | Use of child labour | |
| | | Less education | SDG 4 (Quality Education) |
| | | Health risk | SDG 3 (Good Health), SDG 1 (No Poverty) |
| | | Job-training | SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) |
| | | Wage level --> Discrimination in wage | SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities) |

**Note:** Conventional mining pathway would have its own separate consequences (not shown in detail).

---

### Step 1 (continued): Identify Interlinkages Between SDGs

**Critical Insight:** SDGs are interconnected. Progress or regress on one SDG can affect others.

#### Examples of SDG Interlinkages:

**Example 1: Sustainable Cities --> Life Below Water**
- SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities) impacts:
  - Target 14.2 (Protect and restore ecosystems)
  - Target 14.5 (Conserve coastal and marine areas)

**Example 2: Zero Hunger --> Life on Land**
- Target 2.3 (Double agricultural productivity and small farm income) impacts:
  - SDG 15 (Life on Land) - potential trade-off

**Example 3: Food Access --> Water and Chemicals**
- Target 2.1 (Access to food) impacts:
  - Target 6.4 (Increase water-use efficiency)
  - Target 12.4 (Responsible management of chemicals and waste)

**Example 4: Health --> Energy (bidirectional)**
- Target 3.9 (Reduce death and illness from chemicals and pollution) is influenced by:
  - Target 7.1 (Universal access to modern energy)
  - Target 7.2 (Increase renewable energy percentage)

**Example 5: Health and Gender Equality (bidirectional)**
- Targets 3.1 (Maternal health) and 3.7 (Reproductive health care) have bidirectional links with:
  - Target 5.5 (Full participation in leadership)
  - Target 5.1 (End discrimination against women)

**Example 6: Infrastructure --> Health**
- Target 9.1 (Develop sustainable infrastructure) influences:
  - Target 3.6 (Reduce deaths from road traffic accidents)

### Step 2: Semi-Quantitative Assessment

For each consequence/SDG impact, assess using **three criteria**:

#### The Three Assessment Criteria

| Criterion | Question | Assessment Approach |
|-----------|----------|---------------------|
| **Pos/Neg** | Is the impact positive or negative? | Determine direction of effect |
| **Likelihood** | How likely is it that the impact will happen? | Based on objective data if possible |
| **Size** | What is the expected size of the impact? | Based on objective data if possible |

---

#### Estimating Likelihood

| Category | Description | Probability |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Likely** | Fair ground to believe that the SDG impact will happen | **> 67%** |
| **Possible** | Some possibility to believe that the SDG impact will happen | **33-67%** |
| **Unlikely** | Little possibility to believe that the SDG impact will happen. *If the possibility cannot be estimated, use this category.* | **< 33%** |

---

#### Estimating Size (Magnitude)

| Size Category | Range | Description | Example |
|---------------|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Major** | 50-100% | Effect will have a major impact on the SDG | More electrical cars in renewable energy system --> reduced particle emissions --> large impact on SDG 3 (health) and SDG 11 (sustainable cities) |
| **Moderate** | 20-50% | Effect will have a moderate impact on the SDG | New battery technology will impact SDG 8 (decent jobs) positively, but contribution may be limited compared to full job market |
| **Minor** | 5-20% | Effect will have a minor impact on the SDG | Production of parts for electrical cars --> more emissions to sea (SDG 14) --> but little impact compared to other pollution sources |
| **Negligible** | 0-5% | Effect will have no or negligible impact on the SDG | - |

---

#### The Scoring Matrix

Combine Size and Likelihood to get a final score:

| Size \ Likelihood | **Likely** | **Possible** | **Unlikely** |
|-------------------|------------|--------------|--------------|
| **Major** | +++/--- | ++/-- | +/- |
| **Moderate** | ++/-- | +/- | 0 |
| **Minor** | +/- | 0 | 0 |
| **Negligible** | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**Reading the scores:**
- **+++** = Strong positive impact
- **---** = Strong negative impact
- **++/--** = Moderate impact
- **+/-** = Slight impact
- **0** = Negligible/no impact

---

### Important Assessment Advice

> **If you are convinced that the case is either very positive or very negative... Try to argue for the opposite position!**

This helps ensure balanced, unbiased assessment.

## Phase 5: Interpretation of Results and Recommendations

### Key Questions for Interpretation

1. **Most Important Sustainability Aspects**
   - Which are the most important sustainability aspects?

2. **SDG Impact Analysis**
   - Which SDGs are impacted by the project?
   - In which **life cycle stages** do they appear?
   - **What causes them**?

3. **Interrelations Analysis**
   - Which **interrelations** do you see **between the SDGs**?
   - Do you see consequences **between life cycle stages**?

4. **Improvement Opportunities**
   - **Where** in the life cycle can you introduce change to reduce negative impacts or increase positive impacts?
   - Which **specific changes** could that be?

5. **Recommendations**
   - Which **short-term** recommendations will you make?
   - Which **long-term** recommendations will you make?
   - **Which decision makers** should receive these recommendations?

## SDGUI Tool - Phase 4 Interface

The SDGUI (SDG User Interface) provides a digital platform for completing Phase 4:

### Features:
- **Impact Matrix:** Grid showing all 17 SDGs against effects (E1, E2, E3, etc.)
- **Life Cycle Stages:** Effects organized by Raw materials, Production, Use, Recycling & End-of-Life
- **Impact Scoring:** Visual representation from +++ (top) to --- (bottom)
- **Impact Cards:** For each effect-SDG link, document:
  - SDG and Target number
  - Justification for impact (positive/negative)
  - Justification for likelihood
  - Justification for magnitude
  - Resulting score

### Using SDGUI:
1. Select an effect
2. Drag relevant SDG icon to create a link
3. Fill in justifications for:
   - Impact direction (positive/negative)
   - Likelihood (with justification)
   - Magnitude (with justification)
4. The tool calculates and displays the score
5. Can add consequences as intermediate steps
6. Can duplicate or delete impacts as needed

## Running Example: Urban Farming (Boston Tomatoes)

### Case Description
"The city of Boston in Massachusetts has decided to stop importing tomatoes from Mexico by 2030 and instead grow tomatoes in heated greenhouses on rooftops to cover their own needs year-round."

### Phase 1-3 Analysis Summary

**Functional Output:**
- 700,000 people in Boston by 2030
- Assumption: 1 tomato per person per day
- 1 medium tomato = 123 grams average
- **Annual supply needed: 31,000 tons tomatoes**

**Baseline System:**
- 31,000 tons tomatoes grown in Mexico
- Transportation from Mexico to Boston by truck

**New System:**
- 31,000 tons tomatoes grown in rooftop greenhouses in Boston
- Transportation from roofs to supermarkets

### Process Comparison

**Baseline System Processes (Life Cycle Stages):**

| Materials & Components | Production | Use & Maintenance | Recycling & End-of-Life |
|------------------------|------------|-------------------|-------------------------|
| Harvesting of wood | Production of cardboard | Transport by truck | Waste treatment of left-over tomatoes |
| Production of energy | Assembly of cardboard boxes | Supermarket storage | Waste treatment of packaging |
| Extraction of crude oil | Packaging of tomatoes | Production of energy for cooling | Energy recovery from incineration |
| Refining crude oil for fuel | Harvesting of tomatoes | | |
| Extraction of water for irrigation | Transport of packaging | | |
| Production of fertilizer, pesticide, fungicide | Transport of fertilizer | | |

**New System - Additional Processes:**

| Materials & Components | Production | Use |
|------------------------|------------|-----|
| Mining and production of silica sand | (Same as baseline + greenhouse operations) | Combustion of natural gas for heating |
| Production of glass | | |
| Mining and processing of aluminum ore | | |
| Transport of building materials to site | | |
| Transportation of soil to greenhouse | | |
| Extraction of natural gas | | |

**Key Difference:** The new system eliminates long-distance transport but adds:
- Greenhouse construction (glass, aluminum)
- Year-round heating requirements (natural gas)

### Research Finding
A Journal of Cleaner Production study (Goldstein, Hauschild, et al., 2016) compared urban agriculture with conventional methods and found:
- High-yield, heated greenhouse production of tomatoes has **potentially higher environmental burdens** in terms of **climate change (267-369%)** and non-renewable resource depletion (108-239%)
- Driven primarily by external energy inputs
- "Urban agriculture may not be the optimal application of space in northern cities to improve urban environmental performance"

## Key Definitions Summary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Functional Output** | The quantified service/product that both systems must provide (basis for comparison) |
| **Baseline System** | The existing/current system being replaced |
| **New System** | The proposed alternative system |
| **Effect** | A change resulting from comparing baseline and new systems |
| **Consequence** | An intermediate result linking an effect to an SDG impact |
| **Causality Chain** | The logical pathway from Effect --> Consequences --> SDG Impact |
| **Interlinkage** | Connection between different SDGs where one influences another |
| **Hot Spot** | Area in the life cycle with significant negative sustainability impacts |
| **Semi-quantitative Assessment** | Scoring using categories (Likely/Possible/Unlikely, Major/Moderate/Minor) rather than precise numbers |

## Exam Preparation Checklist

### Phase 4 - Know How To:
- [ ] Construct causality chains linking effects to SDGs
- [ ] Identify direct vs. consequence-mediated SDG impacts
- [ ] Recognize interlinkages between SDGs
- [ ] Apply the likelihood scale (Likely >67%, Possible 33-67%, Unlikely <33%)
- [ ] Apply the size scale (Major 50-100%, Moderate 20-50%, Minor 5-20%, Negligible 0-5%)
- [ ] Use the scoring matrix to combine likelihood and size
- [ ] Document justifications for each assessment

### Phase 5 - Know How To:
- [ ] Identify which SDGs are most impacted and why
- [ ] Analyze impacts across life cycle stages
- [ ] Recognize interrelations between SDGs in your case
- [ ] Identify hot spots for improvement
- [ ] Formulate short-term and long-term recommendations
- [ ] Match recommendations to appropriate decision makers

### Key Concepts to Remember:
- [ ] Life cycle perspective is essential (Materials, Production, Use, End-of-Life)
- [ ] Three sustainability dimensions: Environment, Society, Economy
- [ ] SDGs are interconnected - impacts can cascade
- [ ] Always challenge your initial assumptions (argue the opposite position)
- [ ] Iteration is expected - refine effects and assessments as needed
