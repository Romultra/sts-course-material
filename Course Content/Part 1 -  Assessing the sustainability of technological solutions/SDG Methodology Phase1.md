# SDG Methodology - Phase 1: Defining the Scope and Application

> **Document Context:** This replaces Section 2.1 in the SDG Methodology document. Phase 1 is the first step in the SDG screening assessment process.

---

## Purpose of Phase 1

**Goal:** Clarify the case study's **scope** and **application** before detailed data collection begins.

**Why it matters:** A case describes a shift from a **baseline system** to a **new system** defined by technological differences. The case as presented is typically too broad to assess feasibly - you must select a manageable subset.

---

## The Three Scoping Parameters

### 1. Time Span

**Definition:** The temporal boundaries of your assessment - which points in time you compare.

**Two approaches:**

| Approach | Description | Key Consideration |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Historical comparison** | Compare when old technology dominated vs. when new technology dominates | Establishes clear-cut comparison (avoids mixed technology systems) |
| **Future scenario comparison** | Compare two technologies at the same future point (e.g., 2030) | Must establish comparable frameworks for fair assessment |

**Critical insight:** Societal frameworks change over time.
- *Example:* 20 years ago in Denmark, 1 kWh came mainly from fossil coal; today coal is almost absent.

---

### 2. Application Span

**Definition:** How much of the system you want to cover (the breadth of technological scope).

**The balance problem:**
- **Too broad** = Drown in data, difficult to reach conclusions
- **Too narrow** = Cover only a tiny part of the core technology shift

**Key principle:** Limit yourself to an appropriate **sub-system level**.

---

### 3. Geographical Span

**Definition:** The physical/regional boundaries of your assessment.

**Why necessary:** Technologies may be globally relevant, but you cannot assess the entire world.

**Options (from broad to narrow):**
- Specific country
- Specific region
- Very local area

**Benefits of geographical limitation:**
- Makes data collection feasible
- Enables understanding of specific physical boundaries

---

## Concrete Example: Fossil-Based vs. Bio-Based Chemicals

This example illustrates why scoping is essential:

| What you CANNOT do | What you SHOULD do |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| Assess ALL chemicals from both sources | Select specific chemicals to compare |
| Cover ALL applications of a chemical group | Focus on particular applications |
| Look at use over the last century | Define specific time periods |

---

## Types of Technology Shifts

Technology shifts can manifest in different ways:

1. **Replacement** - New technology replaces existing technology
2. **Introduction** - Technologies that did not exist before enter the market
3. **Need creation** - New technologies create entirely new needs in society and new market demands

---

## Focus of the Assessment

**Priority criteria for selecting applications:**
- Most likely to **enter the market**
- Have the **largest potential impact** on SDGs (society or environment)

---

## Handling Uncertainty

**Key acknowledgment:** Our ability to predict which applications will occur and how effective they will be is **limited**.

**Implications:**
- Choices of applications may involve **large uncertainties**
- Selections may even appear **arbitrary**

**Required response:** **Document transparently** - argue for the different assumptions made.

---

## Phase 1 Output

**Deliverable:** A brief report describing:
1. **Time** scope limitations
2. **Application** scope limitations
3. **Geography** scope limitations

**Nature of the output:**
- Forms the **dynamic basis** for phases 2-5
- Is the **first iteration** of how you approach the full study
- Will be refined as the methodology is **highly iterative**

---

## Key Exam Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Baseline system** | The original/current situation before technology shift |
| **New system** | The situation after the technology shift |
| **Scoping** | The process of limiting assessment boundaries to make study feasible |
| **Iterative methodology** | Approach where initial assumptions are refined throughout the study |
| **Transparent documentation** | Recording and justifying all assumptions made |

---

## Quick Reference: Phase 1 Checklist

- [ ] Define **time span** (historical or future scenario)
- [ ] Define **application span** (appropriate sub-system level)
- [ ] Define **geographical span** (country/region/local)
- [ ] Consider expected **outcomes** of technology shift
- [ ] Translate into **concrete application** in socio-economic systems
- [ ] **Document assumptions** transparently
- [ ] Produce **brief scoping report**
