# Day 11.1: Users, Innovation and Resistance

**Course:** 42620 Science, Technology and Society
**Lecturer:** Tanja Schneider, Associate Professor of Science and Technology Studies, DTU
**Part 2:** Mapping Technological Debates in Society

---

## Session Overview

This session explores why people do not accept or use technologies, what would convince them, and how to engage users in technology development. Key questions addressed:
- What kinds of relations do people have with technologies?
- How can we understand technology non-acceptance and resistance?
- How should users be involved in technology development?

---

## 1. Historical Context: The Weaver's Problem

### The Evolution of Producer-Consumer Relations

**Around 1832:** Most goods produced in one-man shops
- Close personal contact between manufacturer and consumer
- Intimate understanding of customer tastes and desires

**Emergence of Modern Industry:**
- Widening distance between engineers and consumers
- Breach between customer and those guiding institutional destiny

**Weaver's Problem:** How to establish a liaison between designers and social groups, separated by the forces of mass production?

---

## 2. Early Solutions to the Weaver's Problem

### Producers' Responses:
- **Marketing and consumer research** (from street polling to neuromarketing)
- **Industrial design and ergonomics** (improve aesthetic quality of goods, design heuristics)

### Consumers' Responses:
- Buy and replace rather than make and mend
- **Repurposing technologies** (finding new uses)
- Inventing technologies and improving existing ones

---

## 3. Later Solutions: The 1970s Paradigm Shift

### Key Transformation:
From viewing lay experts as **objects** that producers should know better
To seeing lay experts as **subjects** that producers should learn from and collaborate with

### Five Academic Approaches Emerged:

| Approach | Focus |
|----------|-------|
| 1. **Collaborative design** | Users as active design partners |
| 2. **Human-centred design (HCD)** | User contexts and practices |
| 3. **Consumption studies** | How people use and modify technologies |
| 4. **Innovation studies** | User-driven innovation |
| 5. **Science and technology studies** | Social construction of technology |

### Design Evolution:
1. Initially: **Technology-oriented design**
2. Since 1970s: Shift to **user-oriented/centred design**
3. Current: **Lay experts as co-designers** of technologies

---

## 4. Persistent Constraints in Technology Development

Five common problems that persist in technology development:

| Constraint | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Viewing social groups and technology as separate** | Failing to see co-construction |
| **Assuming the user is everybody** | Generic user assumptions |
| **Using the I-methodology** | Designing based on oneself |
| **Technologies and their scripts** | Embedded assumptions about users |
| **Not taking non-acceptance and resistance into account** | Ignoring rejection |

---

## 5. Understanding Non-Acceptance and Resistance

### Key Insights (Wyatt, 2005):
- **Invisible actors** and lay experts often neglected
- Common **false assumption:** non-use or non-acceptance is related to lack of access and skills
- Different forms of non-use and non-acceptance **require different technology development processes and policy responses**

### Conceptualizing (Non)Acceptance:
- View as a **continuum** with degrees and forms of participation that can change
- Consider **temporal and social trajectories** (age, education, geographical mobility, organizational structure)

---

## 6. Wyatt's Categories of Non-Users (Wyatt et al., 2002)

### Distinction: Passive Avoidance vs. Active Resistance

| Category | Definition | Type |
|----------|------------|------|
| **Resisters** | Never use technology because they don't want to | Voluntary non-adoption |
| **Rejecters** | Stopped using voluntarily (boring, expensive, have alternatives) | Voluntary abandonment |
| **Excluded** | Lack access for different reasons | Involuntary non-adoption |
| **Expelled** | Stopped using involuntarily (cost, lost institutional access) | Involuntary abandonment |

**Key Insight:** These categories require **different responses** from designers and policymakers.

## 7. Engineering Practice Implications

### Example 1: Public Infrastructure (Smart Meters)
- **Designed for:** Efficiency
- **Users:** Utility companies, tech-savvy homeowners
- **Non-users:** Tenants without control over installations
- **Issues raised:** Consent, privacy, and power

### Example 2: AI and Automation
- **Users:** Organizations deploying algorithmic decision systems
- **Non-users:** People affected by decisions but unable to contest them
- **Key issue:** Non-users are still impacted

### Key Lesson for Engineers:
> **"If you only design for users, you may unintentionally design *against* others."**

## 8. Case Study: FoodCoach Research Project (2020-2024)

### Project Overview:
- **Interdisciplinary research project** on digital health
- **Focus:** Healthy food shopping and consumption
- **Technology:** Progressive web app analyzing digital shopping receipts
- **Funded by:** Swiss National Science Foundation

### Social Science Team Objectives:
1. Accompany design and development phases
2. Integrate **user and non-user perspectives**
3. Identify **motivations, intentions, drivers and barriers** of potential users and non-users
4. Understand **new or evolving social norms** of food and digital health

### Research Methods:
- Focus groups
- Go-alongs (accompanying users in real settings)
- Thematic analysis

### Six Themes Identified:

1. **Attitudes towards delegating caring work to a machine**
2. **Sensitive boundaries between care and control**
3. **Partial and limited personalisation** of automated dietary tracking systems
4. **Diverse values** at play in making healthy food choices
5. **Trust in institutions** behind the data and technology
6. **Controversial and limited vision** of nutritional standards

### Case Example - Bettina (55 years old):
- Teacher, living with daughter, coeliac and milk intolerant
- Does not believe FoodCoach could "work for me" due to "special needs"
- Would not "like to delegate to anyone" decisions about household eating
- Values her own **dietary, bodily, and sociocultural expertise**

### Conclusions on Delegating Expertise:

1. Taking user and non-user views seriously is a way of **engaging with lay people and re-making expertise**
2. Participants point to **limits of datafication and metrification**
3. Emphasis on what is **neglected:** dietary, bodily, and sociocultural expertise
4. **Beyond nutritionism** (Scrinis, 2013): Broader practice of nutrition and eating is hard to capture in a single score/algorithm

## 9. Approaches to User Involvement

### Human-Centred Design (HCD)

**Key Principles:**
- Investigating users for human-computer interactions
- Technologies need to be **worked into contexts of use** before and during wider uptake

**Requirements:**
- Research into users' contexts and practices
- System evaluation **prior to and after launch**
- New interaction design principles suited to the technology and user

**Methods:**
- Ethnographic observation of real-life settings
- Digital observation methods

### Consumption Studies

**Example - Red Associates on Wearables:**
> "Whether it's Apple, Meta, or someone else who succeeds with the next generation of wearables, success will come from understanding how to get our relationship to the body right. That means understanding culture, fashion, and adornment - how we communicate with our bodies, decorate them, and signal with them."

## 10. Implications for Human-Centred Engineers

### Consider How Lay Experts:
- **Consume** technologies
- **Modify** technologies
- **Domesticate** technologies
- **Design** technologies
- **Reconfigure** technologies
- **Resist** technological development

### Key Insights:

1. **Resistance and non-use can be crucial** factors in eventual modification and improvement of technology

2. Consider lay experts as **co-designers** of technology
   - Example: LEGO Ideas platform where users submit and vote on new product ideas

3. Social groups are **defined and transformed by technology** - the relationship is bidirectional

## Summary: Key Takeaways for Exam

1. **Weaver's Problem:** The challenge of bridging distance between designers and users created by mass production

2. **1970s Shift:** From users as objects to users as subjects who should be collaborated with

3. **Four Categories of Non-Users:** Resisters, Rejecters, Excluded, Expelled - each requiring different responses

4. **Persistent Constraints:** I-methodology, assuming universal users, ignoring scripts, separating technology from society

5. **Different non-use forms require different responses** in technology development and policy

6. **Human-Centred Design:** Technologies must be worked into contexts of use through research and evaluation

7. **Key engineering lesson:** Designing only for users may mean designing against non-users

8. **Lay expertise matters:** Users have dietary, bodily, and sociocultural expertise that automated systems may overlook

9. **Resistance can be productive:** Non-acceptance can lead to technology improvement

10. **Co-design potential:** Lay experts can be active co-designers of technology (e.g., LEGO Ideas)
