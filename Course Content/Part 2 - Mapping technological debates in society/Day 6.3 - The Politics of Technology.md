# Day 6.3 - The Politics of Technology

---

This lecture explores the politics of technology, showing how differing interests, experiences, and social positions make emerging technologies controversial. It challenges three common myths—autonomy, value-neutrality, and inherent goodness—by introducing the concepts of interpretative flexibility, script, and political technologies, which reveal how design choices and user perspectives shape technological impacts. More details are provided in the attached lecture note and the further readings listed below.



# Further readings

## *For a general introduction into the topic:*

Feng P (2000) Rethinking technology, revitalizing ethics: Overcoming barriers to ethical design. *Science and Engineering Ethics* 6(2): 207–220.

## *For an introduction into the specific concepts discussed above:*

For the concept of script: Akrich M (1992) The De-Scription of Technical Objects. In: Bijker WE and Law J (eds) *Shaping Technology/Building Society: Studies in Sociotechnical Change*. Inside technology. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, pp. 205–224.

For the concept of interpretative flexibility: Kline R and Pinch T (1996) Users as Agents of Technological Change: The Social Construction of the Automobile in the Rural United States. *Technology and Culture* 37(4): 763–795.

For the concept of technological politics: Winner L (1980) Do Artifacts Have Politics? *Daedalus* 109(1): 121–136.
