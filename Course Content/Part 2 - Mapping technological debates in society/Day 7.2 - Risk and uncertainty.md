# Day 7.2 - Risk and uncertainty

---

Risk and uncertainty are both key to RRI. Trying to anticipate the intended and unintended ways in which emerging technologies have implications for society naturally concerns the risk such technologies pose - i.e. how likely certain adverse consequences of different levels of severity are to happen - and the uncertainty surrounding such estimates.

However, even if the risk of something bad happening can be objectively measured (assuming uncertainty is limited), it is still the case that different people have different risk perceptions, i.e. different ideas about how risk should be handled. We work with the **cultural theory of risk** and the **grid/group model** to explore different risk perceptions and their consequences. The model was originally developed by British anthropologist Mary Douglas and has since been applied to a range of technological contexts spanning from radiation hazards in hospitals to climate change.



### Further readings and resources:

*A widely cited review of the cultural theory of risk and its applications:*

Tansey, J., & O'riordan, T. (1999). Cultural theory and risk: a review. Health, risk & society, 1(1), 71-90.

*An essay on the foundations of the grid / group model by its inventor Mary Douglas:*

Douglas, M. (2007). A history of grid and group cultural theory. Toronto, Canada: University of Toronto.
