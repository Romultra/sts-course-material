# Day 8.1 - Introduction to controversy mapping

---

The four mapping lectures and assignment D gives you the practical foundation for working with the [dataset of actor statements](https://learn.inside.dtu.dk/d2l/common/dialogs/quickLink/quickLink.d2l?ou=294618&type=content&rcode=dtu-713211) and the principles for good controversy mapping practice that you need to follow in the methodology for your [policy brief](https://learn.inside.dtu.dk/d2l/common/dialogs/quickLink/quickLink.d2l?ou=294618&type=dropbox&rcode=dtu-709184). The **goal** of this introductory lecture is to explain the basic principles of controversy mapping and introduce you to the dataset.

Controversy mapping is the main method we use on the course to address the engagement part of the AREA framework. According to RRI, we are supposed to open anticipation and reflection to a wider range of societal stakeholders. But who are they? How do we discover them? And what are they concerned about?



In this introductory lecture I provide some examples of how controversy mapping uses digital methods in an exploratory way to trace the actors and issues that emerge from ongoing controversies. This will set the stage for the rest of the lectures and workshop activities on Wednesday and Thursday, where Mathieu Jacomy takes you through the practical steps of controversy mapping with the Energy Island Issue Atlas.
