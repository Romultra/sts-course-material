# PART 2: Mapping technological debates in society

---

Part 2 of the course starts on Monday 11/8. We focus on the analysis of technological controversies - i.e. the way actors in society engage and debate technological issues - and how to foster Responsible Research and Innovation (RRI) in that context.

**Transition from Part 1 to Part 2**

Whereas Part 1 have given you tools to assess the sustainability of proposed engineering solutions, Part 2 will help you include and understand diverse stakeholder perspectives on those solutions.

**Goal**

The goal is to help you map the Danish energy island controversy, write a policy brief with RRI recommendations as the first part your exam (submitted on the last day of the course) and complete the multiple choice test as the second part of your exam (also completed on the last day of the course). The mandatory assignments in Part 2 (C to F) are there to support you in that process.

We will build on the proposals for improved sustainability you have brought with you from Part 1 (Assignment B) and work with both theoretical perspectives and methodological approaches to understand how and why technology becomes controversial in society, as well as strategies for mitigating that responsibly.

**Organization**

Part 2 is organized in 1 hr 50 mins sessions. Each session begins with a short lecture followed by exercises with support from TAs in class and feedback with the teacher.

We aim to provide **lecture notes** for every session with the most important concepts, definitions and applications. Together with **slides,** **lecture attendance**, and **participation in exercises** in class, this should be enough to accomplish the learning objectives and complete all the assignments. **Further readings** are listed for the convenience of those who are interested in exploring further on their own initiative.
